# Sukusho 1.3.0 beta 2

The second beta of Sukusho 1.3.0: Saved works as an archive, clipboard pictures keep their own copy, the Library selects, copies and previews the same way on every page, cards have a new layout, and shortcuts can be a modifier key on its own. Open the DMG and drag Sukusho to Applications.

Tested on macOS 26.5.1 and 27.2.1.

**Getting this beta:** with beta 1 installed and **Settings › About › Get beta updates** on (it starts on in a beta), Sukusho offers beta 2 by itself. From 1.2.0, download it here.

## Before you update: what the first launch does
- **A one-time copy of clipboard pictures.** In beta 1, an image in clipboard history that came from a screenshot was the screenshot file itself. The first time beta 2 opens, it copies each of those pictures into clipboard history, once. This takes extra disk space, about the size of those screenshots. The copies don't count toward the history's size limit, so the upgrade doesn't make its clean-up remove anything. A clip whose screenshot was already deleted can't be copied, and stays in history without a picture.
- **Saved now works as an archive** (below). Captures beta 1 put in Saved only because you shared them to Cloud leave Saved; they stay in the Library and keep their links. Captures you saved yourself stay saved.
- Snippets beta 1 let you pin stay in **Snippets**, not Saved.

## New since beta 1

### Saved is an archive
- **Move to Saved** takes a capture or clip out of All Captures, Screenshots, Recordings and Clipboard and keeps it in **Library › Saved**, where clipboard history's clean-up never removes it. It works on several items at once.
- **⌘Z** undoes Move to Saved, and **⇧⌘Z** redoes it.
- Only Move to Saved saves something now. Naming, tagging or sharing a capture no longer does.
- In the clipboard popup, the **Saved** chip shows your saved clips, which you can search like the rest, and **⌘.** moves the selected clip to Saved.

### Clipboard pictures are their own
- A clip of a screenshot keeps its own copy of the picture. Deleting or editing the screenshot no longer changes or empties the clip.
- Double-clicking an image clip opens it in the editor: its screenshot while that exists, otherwise a copy, which becomes a new screenshot when you save.
- An image clip with no name or description is titled by its size, such as "1440 × 900". Pasted or dragged into Finder, it's a file named after it, such as "Image (1440 × 900).png".

### Selecting, copying and Quick Look in the Library
- Clipboard, Snippets and Saved select like Captures: **⌘-click**, **Shift-click**, drag a rectangle, or **⌘A**.
- **⌘C** and **Edit › Copy** work on every page, and copy every selected item, in order.
- Several clips can be shared, exported or shared to Cloud together.
- **Quick Look** (Space) follows the selection on every page, and the arrow keys step through it.
- **Return** renames a capture or snippet, opens an image clip in the editor, copies other clips, opens a Cloud item's page, and downloads a received file.
- Deleting asks first, with **Cancel** as the default, in the Library and in the popup (**⌃X** and **⌘Delete**). A capture moved to the Trash keeps its Cloud link.
- Context menus list what the selection can do, and saved captures get the full capture menu.
- Every card page shares one **Grid** or **List** choice; List hides the size slider. **⌘R** refreshes the page you're on.
- A search or filter with no results says so and offers **Clear Filters**.

### Library cards
- The picture is the same height on every page, with the card's marks in its four corners.
- Every card has a two-line caption: what it is, then the app it came from, the date and ★ if it's saved.
- On narrow cards, the less important marks make room first, and titles end with "…".
- Shell scripts and JSON show as code, and tall clipboard images get the scrolling badge, as screenshots do.

### Shortcuts
- A shortcut can be **any one modifier with a key** (⌥1, ⌃X, ⇧A), or **F1 to F20** on its own. When it also types a character, Settings says so under its row.
- A shortcut can be **a modifier key on its own**, tapped once (**Right ⌥**) or twice (**⌥ ⌥**, **⌘ ⌘**). To record one, click the shortcut, wait a moment with no key held, then tap. Menus show it after the title, as in **Capture Area (Right ⌥)**.
- A modifier key on its own needs **Input Monitoring**, so Sukusho can notice it in other apps; it never records what you type. Settings says when it's missing, opens the right place in System Settings, and offers **Relaunch Sukusho**, since macOS applies the permission only after a relaunch.
- A double tap that also starts macOS Dictation says so under its row.
- While you record a shortcut, the others pause, so a shortcut that's already in use shows which action has it instead of running.

### Clipboard popup
- **⌘1** to **⌘0** paste the first ten clips shown, in the order you see them.
- The popup follows **Settings › General › Include Sukusho windows in captures** in Sukusho's own screenshots and recordings, like Sukusho's other windows.
- The empty Snippets page names your own shortcut for the popup.

### Settings and Studio
- **Settings › General** lets you choose a folder for videos.
- Uploading to Cloud after a capture stays off, and says why, until Cloud is set up.
- The Cloud setup video says when it can't load, with **Retry**, and the steps still work offline.
- Apps without an icon get a stand-in in the ignored-apps list, and long version numbers wrap.
- Studio's first window fits the screen, inspector included.

## Fixes
- Copy was unavailable for a selected clip in the Library.
- Double-clicking an older clip moved it to the top of the history.
- Quick Look kept showing an earlier item after the selection changed.
- Recording a shortcut that was already in use ran that shortcut.
- The notice about macOS's screenshot shortcuts counted as answered when it was closed without an answer. It now comes back at the next launch, names the actions that kept their keys, and **Open Keyboard Shortcuts…** opens Keyboard Shortcuts, where you choose Screenshots on the left.
